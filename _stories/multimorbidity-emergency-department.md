## The question

Many people who attend emergency departments live with several long-term conditions at once, known as multimorbidity. How does that affect what happens to them afterwards?

## What we did

As part of the NIHR-funded AIM-CISC project, colleagues led by the University of Edinburgh, with us, studied 451,291 people who made 1,273,937 emergency department attendances. Multimorbidity meant at least two conditions from a standard list. We looked at 30-day mortality, hospital admission, return within seven days and time spent in the department.

## What we found

About one in ten patients, 9.6%, had multimorbidity. They were older, more often arrived by emergency ambulance and were more often triaged as very urgent. After adjustment, multimorbidity was linked to higher 30-day mortality (8.2% versus 1.2%), more hospital admissions (60.1% versus 20.5%), more returns within seven days and longer stays. The associations were stronger in younger people: for 30-day mortality the adjusted odds ratio was 3.03 for those under 65 and 1.61 for those 65 or older.

## Why it matters

Multimorbidity is common in emergency care and strongly linked to poor outcomes, and it is increasing, so services need to plan for it.
