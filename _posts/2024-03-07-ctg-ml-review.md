title: Paper published in *Computers in Biology and Medicine* – *Machine learning on cardiotocography data to classify fetal outcomes: A scoping review* – at [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compbiomed.2024.108220](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compbiomed.2024.108220)  <br/><br/> Cardiotocography (CTG) monitoring is widely used in labour to detect fetal hypoxia, but its clinical usefulness is limited by the relatively poor positive predictive value of an abnormal CTG and by significant variability between and within observers in how traces are interpreted. Machine learning (ML) has been applied to CTG classification since the early 2000s and has shown potential to predict fetal hypoxia more accurately than visual interpretation alone. To consider how these tools might be translated into clinical practice, we reviewed the ML techniques already applied to CTG classification and identified research gaps that need to be addressed before clinical implementation. We followed PRISMA-ScR and searched PubMed, EMBASE and IEEE Xplore, and included 36 studies that used signal processing and ML to classify CTG. Most used an open-access CTG database and predominantly used fetal metabolic acidosis, with varying pH levels, as the benchmark for hypoxia. We identified significant concerns over the practicality of using varying pH levels as the CTG classification benchmark, and studies needed to be more generalised, as most used the same database with a low number of subjects for an ML study. ML studies demonstrate potential in predicting fetal hypoxia from CTG. However, more diverse datasets, standardisation of hypoxia benchmarks and enhancement of algorithms and features are needed for future clinical implementation.<br/><br/>Read the full paper at [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compbiomed.2024.108220](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compbiomed.2024.108220).
